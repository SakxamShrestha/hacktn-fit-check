Get help

The questions that come up most often, on both sides of a donation.

If you are donating

What do I need before I start?

The rating plate is enough. It is the stamped metal plate on the back of the machine or beside the power inlet, and it carries the maker, the model, the voltage, the frequency and usually the year.

What if I cannot photograph it?

Paste whatever you have instead — a specification sheet, the label text, or what your biomedical department told you. Anything you leave out stays marked unknown and comes back as a question rather than a guess.

Am I committing to send it?

No. Posting an offer puts it in front of clinics that can actually run it. You are asked to confirm the stock still exists before anything moves, and saying no is useful — the clinic is told straight away.

Why was my device rejected?

It was not rejected; it was found not to fit that clinic. The verdict names the check that blocked it. The same device may be fine somewhere else, and often is.

If you need equipment

Why do you ask about my building?

Because that is what decides whether a machine works. Voltage, frequency, outages, ward temperature, humidity, oxygen, compressed air, distilled water and who can maintain it. You enter it once.

Can I upload the list I already keep?

Yes. A spreadsheet or CSV with a column of equipment names, and a quantity column if you keep one. You can also just write out what you need in ordinary sentences.

Why is an item greyed out?

It did not pass screening for your facility, and the reason is printed beside it. We leave it visible rather than hiding it, so you can see what would have to change for it to work.

Nothing I asked for is available.

Where a clinically equivalent device exists and passes, it is offered underneath with the reason it can stand in. A substitute that also fails is never offered.

What happens after I choose?

We confirm availability with the donor and come back to you. Nothing is reserved until a donor has said the stock is still there.
