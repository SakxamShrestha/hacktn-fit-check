About Fit-Check

We screen donated medical equipment against the conditions of the clinic receiving it, before anyone books a container.

The problem

A great deal of donated medical equipment arrives and never works. Not because it is broken, but because it does not fit where it was sent.

A 110 volt ventilator on a 240 volt grid. A machine that needs piped oxygen sent to a clinic with cylinders. A unit rated to 40°C in a ward that reaches 42°C. Sterile stock that expires in the container.

Each of those is knowable in advance. Nobody checks, because checking means holding the specification of the device against the real conditions of the building, and that has always been done by hand.

How it works

A donor photographs the rating plate or pastes the specification. A model reads the facts off it and labels each one with where it came from — the plate, a document, the standard, or nobody.

The decision is not made by the model. Fifteen constraint checks written as ordinary code compare those facts against the clinic's recorded conditions. Given the same facts they return the same verdict every time, which is why a verdict cannot be invented.

Anything blocked stays visible with the reason. A screen is not a clearance: a person still decides.

What we check

Power

Mains voltage, frequency, supply phase, and how often the grid fails.

Environment

Ward temperature and humidity against the device's rated range.

Utilities

Medical oxygen, compressed air and distilled water.

Support

Consumables, spare parts, manufacturer support and shelf life on arrival.

What we are built on

The acceptance criteria in the World Health Organization's guidance on medical device donations, Annex 1. The marking requirements and default environmental conditions in IEC 60601-1. Published grid data for 243 countries, and the United States FDA device registry.

Fit-Check is a screening tool, not a regulator, a shipper or a clinical authority. We do not take custody of equipment and we do not arrange freight.
